# 1. Problem framing

Card fraud detection is not an offline accuracy contest. It is a decision made under two hard constraints: a fixed review capacity, because analysts can only work so many alerts a day, and an asymmetric cost, because a missed fraud costs a chargeback while a false positive costs a good customer. At the fraud rates of real portfolios, around 0.17% in the benchmark used here, a model that optimizes accuracy is worthless and a model that optimizes ranking is only useful if its ranking holds at the top of the list, where the review budget actually binds.

HSBC's problem statement asks whether quantum methods can improve detection in digital payment ecosystems. We took that question literally and built the apparatus to answer it in the form a bank could act on: a working classical detector, a quantum-inspired candidate evaluated against it under a protocol frozen before any result was seen, and a measured verdict reported in both directions.

This proposal states what we built, what we measured on real quantum hardware, what the measurement means, and what a 90-day production trial would run.

# 2. Technical approach

**A note on language.** We present a classical boosting formulation whose training step is solved on QCi's Dirac-3 entropy quantum computing hardware. It did not beat the tuned classical detector on the primary endpoint, so we do not use the phrase quantum-enhanced, and we reserve any claim of enhancement for a result measured against a strong classical control.

**Paradigm**: hybrid classical-quantum. Weak-learner construction and all inference are classical; Dirac-3 performs a photonic quantum optimization step at training time, with a classical convex solve of the identical objective as the control. QCi's hardware paper describes Dirac-3 as a hybrid photonic-electronic entropy quantum computer, which supports calling it quantum hardware; it does not establish quantum advantage, and we claim none. No gate-based experiment is claimed here: a Phase 2 Amazon Braket arm would be evaluated only if a circuit and a matched classical baseline pass a classical feasibility screen first.

**The system.** A tuned gradient-boosted ensemble (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost, each given an equal 100-trial Optuna budget) is the production detector. CVQBoost is the evaluated candidate: it trains a pool of weak classifiers on small feature subsets, one depth-limited decision tree per feature and per correlation-ranked feature pair, each emitting a hard vote in {-1, +1} with no class weighting or subsampling in the frozen configuration, then selects ensemble weights by minimizing a quadratic objective on the simplex, which Dirac-3 solves natively. The method admits one-, two-, and three-feature subsets; every run reported here used one- and two-feature subsets, giving 91 weak learners over 13 features, because three-feature subsets would require 377 variables, beyond the Phase 1 allocation we had budgeted for pool construction and metered time. QCi's documented 949 limit is a sum over num_levels for integer-encoded jobs and does not bind our continuous runs; what binds them is a separate free-tier ceiling of 100 variables, established empirically when a 312-variable job was refused server-side. The frozen 91-variable configuration sits just under that ceiling, so no earlier run hit it. Appendix B gives the bounds. A third arm, the exact classical solve of the identical objective, serves as a structural control.

**The protocol is the contribution.** Before running anything we froze a preregistration: hypotheses, gates with numeric pass criteria, splits, seeds, metrics, statistical procedures, and the hardware budget. Changes since the freeze exist only as nineteen dated amendments, each with its rationale and approval, published in Appendix B. Concretely this means: gates are scored as committed even when they fail; every number carries an evidence tag ([HW] measured on hardware, [SIM] simulated or classical, [PROJ] projected); every number traces to a results record by configuration hash; leakage controls are enforced in code paths rather than by review; and a shuffled-label control confirms the pipeline collapses to chance when the signal is removed.

That discipline is why the results below can be trusted in the direction that is inconvenient for us.

# 3. What we measured

All figures: ULB benchmark, 284,807 transactions, 1,081 exact duplicates removed before splitting, 60/20/20 stratified, ten seeds, test-fold prevalence 0.00167. Full tables in Appendix A.

**Classical baselines are clean and strong.** CatBoost leads at 0.8368 AUPRC [SIM] (95% CI 0.8150 to 0.8585), with XGBoost and LightGBM within 0.013. The shuffled-label control collapses to 0.0023 against a base rate of 0.0017, and no cell approaches the 0.95 anomaly tripwire. High AUPRC is not itself evidence of leakage; the controls that do the work are splitting before preprocessing, train-only tuning, deduplication, and the shuffled-label test.

**Gate G0 failed as committed.** Our preregistered floor of 0.85 AUPRC for tuned XGBoost was not met: 0.8296 [SIM]. Primary-source research afterwards found the 0.85 to 0.88 band uncorroborated: clean-protocol equivalents fall near 0.80 and a step-wise-metric benchmark reports 0.78, both of which we exceed. The gate still reads FAIL, because moving a threshold after seeing the result is what preregistration exists to prevent.

**Gate G0b passed on hardware.** The gate ranks five paired configurations, and its Spearman of 0.900 [HW] is therefore over n = 5 (exact permutation p = 0.042 one-sided), which is why we treat it as a smoke test and not as evidence of numerical agreement. The campaign behind it is 37 metered Dirac-3 fits over 163 metered device seconds in three blocks -- 22 B1, 5 G0b, 10 mixed-pool -- with zero failures and zero retries. We describe it for what it is, and qualify it in Appendix A.3: a smoke test licensing proxy-based development, not validation of numerical agreement.

**The primary hypothesis returned a null.** CVQBoost on Dirac-3 trails the best matched-feature GBDT by 0.0399 AUPRC [HW] (CI -0.0571 to -0.0227), beyond our minimum detectable effect of 0.0268, trailing on nine of ten seeds. The across-seed intervals we report measure split dispersion rather than sampling error, since the ten splits resample the same 283,726 rows and their test folds overlap; we label them accordingly. The direction does not depend on that interval: nine of ten seeds trail. We report the corresponding two-sided sign test p of about 0.021 as descriptive only, since the ten resplits share rows and are not independent replicates. Six of ten per-seed paired bootstrap intervals exclude zero, all in the same direction [SIM]; those intervals are computed from proxy scores, for the reason given below.

**Why, precisely, and the control that settles it.** The CVQBoost objective is a convex quadratic on the simplex: the Gram matrix of weak-learner outputs is positive semidefinite by construction and the penalty term is positive, so the optimum is unique and reachable in polynomial time by a high-accuracy numerical solve (FISTA, convergence tolerance 1e-10 on the relative objective). That is a theorem, not a measurement, and we state it before measuring: our hardware-versus-proxy agreement is a confirmation of it rather than a discovery.

The stronger finding is what the optimum turns out to be. **It is uniform to seven decimal places.** Across all ten seeds the solved weight vector sits within an L1 distance of 1.6e-07 of 1/91 on every learner [SIM]. We therefore ran the control that this demands: scoring the same pool with uniform weights by construction gives 0.7659 AUPRC against the solved 0.7681. That residual 0.0022 is not a gain. Uniform weights leave 95.3% of test transactions tied on a single score, and a weight perturbation of order 1e-07 splits those ties, raising the number of distinct scores from 78 to 151; average precision is rank-based, so it moves. Rounding the solved scores back to six decimals returns the metric to the uniform value. **The optimization step contributes nothing on this configuration: what looks like a gain is tie-breaking among transactions the model cannot distinguish, and neither a quantum nor a classical solver is doing useful work.**

The cause is measured directly at the level of the pool, not inferred from the objective. Off-diagonal Gram entries average 170,234.4 against a diagonal of 170,235, so any two learners agree on 99.999% of training rows. At 0.17% prevalence a depth-limited tree predicts the negative class almost everywhere, so the pool carries almost no diversity for a weighting to exploit. A class-weighted re-solve, weighting fraud rows by inverse prevalence, moves the optimum by an L1 distance of 0.002 and leaves it uniform. We state the scope of that control precisely: it rules out the weighting of the ensemble objective, and it does not rule out imbalance effects during weak-learner fitting, which is a different intervention and one the Phase 2 pool work addresses directly. We measured this rather than asserting it: hardware minus exact proxy is -0.0010 AUPRC [HW] with the interval containing zero, solution weights agree to cosine 0.975 to 0.999, and hardware objective values are always above the exact minimum, never below, by margins of 0.013% to 0.413% of the objective value. Dirac-3 is stochastic: no fit returned identical energies across its eight samples, yet the within-fit spread has a median of 0.019% [HW], so sampling variation barely moves the answer near this flat optimum. Nor is there a speed argument at this size: a fit bills 4 to 5 seconds against milliseconds for the exact classical solve. A regularization sweep confirms it from another direction: the optimum stays uniform even at zero penalty, so the penalty term is not the cause either.

The regularization sweep is exploratory, not the preregistered structural control: the two committed controls have not been run, so H4 is reported partial rather than scored.

This is a negative result about one formulation, not about the approach. We then tested the formulation it points at.

**We built the pool the diagnosis called for, and the optimizer started working.** Under a dated amendment, labeled exploratory, four learner families (decision tree, linear discriminant, logistic, k-nearest neighbour) replace the single family on identical splits, seeds and metrics. The optimum leaves uniform: L1 distance 0.126 against 8.0e-08, largest weight 1.24 times uniform against 1.0000000, solved-minus-uniform +0.0076 on ten of ten seeds against +0.0022 [SIM]. Two seed-42 controls show this is optimization rather than tie-breaking (Appendix A.4). The +0.0076 is across ten seeds; those checks are not, and we say so. **The size, plainly: +0.0076 is well below our preregistered minimum detectable effect of 0.0268, so this is a direction with a measured mechanism, not a win.** One fact belonged in the same breath: that pool's absolute AUPRC was 0.7466, BELOW the frozen pool's 0.7681 at thirteen features. Diversity bought the optimizer something to do, not accuracy.

**So we tuned it, and the accuracy came from the learners rather than the optimizer.** Adding imbalance handling AT FIT TIME (class weighting inside the tree and logistic learners, which no earlier control varied: the Sprint 5 control reweighted only the ensemble objective) makes the pool genuinely diverse, with the Gram off-diagonal ratio falling from 0.9988 to 0.92. Two of the four families, LDA and k-nearest neighbours, take no class weight, and the selected configuration also uses closer, distance-weighted neighbours, so the accuracy figure below reflects both changes rather than class weighting alone; the diversity figure is attributable to class weighting, since the variant differing only in neighbours shows the same 0.92. Against the same single-family pool rebuilt at the SAME 6-feature size on the SAME splits, the tuned pool gains **+0.0319 AUPRC, positive on ten of ten seeds, which exceeds our 0.0268 minimum detectable effect** [SIM]. We report the matched comparison rather than the flattering one, and state what it costs us: the frozen pool scores 0.7381 at six features against 0.7681 at thirteen, so six features are materially worse for it and any raw cross-size comparison would be inflated. The +0.0319 is the paired per-seed difference at matched size, which is why it is the number we report. **This is the first difference in the project to clear that threshold**, and it did so only after we corrected a protocol violation in our own code (A17) -- a direction of movement that should invite checking, which the committed generator and per-seed values allow. The optimizer's own contribution stays at +0.0047, well below the threshold. The accuracy came from better weak learners, not from better optimization, which is a statement about where the value sits in this formulation. Dispersion for both: the learner effect is +0.0319 (SD 0.0189), the optimizer's +0.0047 (SD 0.0038), each on ten of ten seeds; only the first clears the MDE. At 0.7700 the tuned arm exceeds the frozen CVQBoost pool at matched size by a measured margin, while still trailing both the 0.8 that Loke et al. report and the tuned classical ensemble.

**Independent evidence pointing the same way.** Loke, Sahoo, Guan, Xu, Verma and Griffin (ICAART 2026, Singapore Management University) ran CVQBoost on the same Dirac-3 hardware against the same benchmark and reported mean AUC-PR above 0.8, against our 0.767. The design difference is precisely the pool: their weak learners are k-nearest neighbours, linear discriminant analysis, logistic regression and XGBoost, a heterogeneous set whose members fail on different transactions, where ours are 91 depth-limited decision trees that agree on 99.999% of rows. We did not find this paper before freezing, and it is the closest prior work that exists. It does not overturn our result; it explains it, and it converts our Phase 2 direction from an inference into a replication target with a published number attached.

That points at heterogeneous learner families rather than one family over many feature subsets, feature subsets chosen for decorrelation, imbalance handling during learner fitting rather than only in the ensemble objective, and the sparse-selection formulation in section 6, which is the one that would force the optimizer to choose rather than average.

**Two facts about the selected configuration that a bank would want stated together.** It carries a score-degeneracy warning on all ten seeds (95.1% of transactions share one score), so its ranking metrics are sound while its threshold-dependent figures are weak evidence. And on the single time-ordered split it falls to 0.7095 while the 9-feature configuration we did not select rises to 0.7776 [HW] -- selection followed the preregistered validation-only rule and was not revisited afterwards. IEEE-CIS now supplies the proper temporal test that ULB's two-day span cannot.

**In operational terms the gap is smaller than the metric suggests.** At a 0.1% review budget the production detector, all-feature CatBoost, catches 59.3% of test-fold fraud; the CVQBoost arm catches 56.5% [SIM], a 2.8 point difference. Against the matched-13 CatBoost that H1b uses as its comparator the figure is 58.0%. One caveat belongs in the same breath: that budget funds 57 alerts against about 95 frauds, so attainable recall is capped at 0.600 and CatBoost reaches 98.8% of the ceiling. The narrow gap is therefore partly budget saturation, not only model quality. The 0.5% budget is the only unsaturated column we have, and there the figures are 85.5% and 81.9%, a 3.6 point difference. These are proxy-derived [SIM]: the Sprint 4 campaign did not persist Dirac-3 weights. We closed that gap with a ten-fit metered block that persisted every weight and score, and we state plainly that it ran a SMALLER pool than the mechanism result above: the free-tier ceiling of 100 variables forecloses the 312-variable 13-feature configuration, so the hardware block used 6 features and 60 variables. Both counts are four learner families over pairs only: 4 x 78 pairs at 13 features, 4 x 15 at 6. On that configuration hardware AUPRC is 0.7630, at a 0.1% budget it catches 50.9% of fraud at 88.4% precision, hardware minus proxy is -0.0007 and weight cosine 0.977 to 0.983 [HW]. What this licenses is the proxy substitution itself, measured on the same device; it is not a hardware confirmation of the 13-feature mechanism result, which remains [SIM] and which the free tier cannot run.

# 4. Production trial and expected impact

**A 90-day shadow-mode trial.** Days 1-30: the tuned classical ensemble scores live traffic in shadow, deciding nothing, while its alerts are compared against the incumbent's on the same transactions. The comparison metric is the one the fraud team already uses, fraud caught within the standing review budget, not AUPRC. Days 31-60: champion/challenger, with the challenger taking a small routed share of decisions under a rollback trigger tied to precision at the alert budget. Days 61-90: the quantum-inspired arm enters the same shadow track as a specialist candidate, evaluated on the segments identified in Phase 2 rather than on aggregate performance.

**What the trial must satisfy to proceed.** Latency: real-time scoring budgets are in the low tens of milliseconds, a Phase 2 target to be confirmed by p50/p95/p99 measurement rather than a figure we have measured, which the classical ensemble meets and which the CVQBoost arm meets trivially at inference, since inference is a weighted vote over small classifiers; the quantum solve happens at training time only. Calibration: the downstream rules engine consumes a score, so the model ships with a fitted calibration layer and reliability diagrams with equal-mass binning, specified for the trial and not fitted in Phase 1. Drift: decay rates vary by product, geography and fraud type, so the trial estimates them from portfolio data rather than assuming a fixed horizon, and defines retraining triggers on population stability and on precision-at-budget degradation rather than on a fixed calendar. Our own temporal check is a warning worth naming precisely. On a single time-ordered split the two configurations swapped places, and the benchmark spans only two days, so this tests intra-day ordering rather than weeks-scale concept drift: the configuration we selected fell from 0.7671 to 0.7095 AUPRC, while the configuration we rejected rose from 0.7014 to 0.7776 [HW]. One split proves nothing and we claim nothing from it, but it points the wrong way for our own choice, and establishing which configuration survives time ordering is the first thing the trial should measure.

**Explainability, which is the bar that actually gates adoption.** A CVQBoost ensemble is a weighted vote over classifiers that each read one to three named features, so a decision decomposes into exact arithmetic terms rather than a post-hoc approximation. Because the benchmark's features are anonymized principal components, this is an exact audit decomposition rather than a customer- or investigator-meaningful explanation; that gap closes only on named bank features. We measured this on a real flagged fraud, with the worked decomposition in the repository: the score is the sum of per-learner contributions, each naming its feature pair and its weight. The honest counterpart, also measured: at the current configuration weight is spread widely, taking 46 of 91 learners to reach half the total, so the explanation is exact but long. A sparser formulation would shorten it, which is one more reason it is the Phase 2 direction.

**Two requirements we have not yet met, stated rather than omitted.** Fairness testing across protected characteristics is absent from this work. UK firms must avoid unlawful indirect discrimination under the Equality Act 2010, and FCA Consumer Duty guidance identifies algorithmic bias producing systematically worse outcomes as a source of harm; neither mandates one named statistical test, so disparity testing is the governance control we propose rather than a cited requirement. the benchmark's anonymized features make it untestable here, and it becomes a first-class requirement the moment real customer data is in scope. Label latency is the second: chargeback labels arrive weeks after the transaction, so the trial's evaluation window must account for censored labels rather than treating recent unlabeled transactions as legitimate.

**Expected impact, quantified.** Under the trial's own metric the decision is whether a candidate arm raises fraud caught within a fixed analyst budget, so the impact of any recall difference is arithmetic. For a portfolio processing one million transactions a day at the benchmark's 0.17% fraud rate, a 0.1% review budget funds 1,000 alerts against roughly 1,700 frauds, which caps attainable recall at 58.8% before any model is chosen; within that ceiling each percentage point of recall is about 17 additional frauds caught per day [PROJ]. The 2.8 point gap we measure between the production detector and the current quantum-inspired arm is therefore roughly 48 frauds a day at that scale, which is the number a trial would have to move. We give this as an illustration of the arithmetic, not a forecast: it assumes a portfolio we have not seen, and the benchmark spans two days.

Our current measurement says the classical ensemble is the benchmark champion, the quantum arm is within three percentage points of caught fraud without yet having its best formulation, and a router combining them is a Phase 2 hypothesis with an acceptance criterion rather than a proven design. The champion is not deployable on bank traffic as fitted: the benchmark's PCA transform is not published, so onboarding requires feature mapping, retraining, temporal validation, calibration and governance approval before any live comparison.

**The first thing Phase 2 should resolve** is the temporal split reversal described in section 3. The configuration our preregistered rule selected degrades under time ordering while the one it rejected improves, and fraud models are deployed into a moving distribution. Until that is settled with a proper temporal protocol rather than one split, no configuration of this arm should be recommended for a production decision path.

# 5. Hybrid integration

The deployed shape is a classical detector with a quantum-inspired specialist behind a router. The classical ensemble scores every transaction. The specialist would be invoked only on segments where it is measured to help, at training-time cost only, since its inference is a linear vote. No result here identifies such a segment; the router is accepted in Phase 2 only if prespecified segments show out-of-time incremental lift over the classical champion against a matched random-segment control. The quantum hardware sits at training and model-selection time, where its solve bills 4 to 5 metered device seconds per fit, with end-to-end wall time additionally covering request, queue and response handling, and it sits outside the transaction path entirely. This division is why the hybrid is deployable at all: nothing in the live path depends on a quantum device being reachable.

# 6. Phase 2 program

Four levers remain open, each with a preregistered test already written.

**Representation, now measured.** Quantum feature engineering applied identically to every arm, with order-matched classical twins, asked whether a phase representation shifts the quantum-minus-classical difference. It does not, by an amount we can claim: across ten seeds the shift is **-0.0115 against a paired SD of 0.0135**, which is 43% of our 0.0268 minimum detectable effect [SIM] (Appendix A.6). The direction is negative on seven of ten seeds, which hints that the classical bar uses the added representation slightly better, but that is below what we preregistered as detectable and we do not report it as a finding. The twins are what make the question answerable: a trained-frequency GAM in the classical bar scores 0.7893 against the quantum arm's 0.7646, so the comparison is against a model that can also exploit periodic structure. Omitting such twins is how apparent quantum wins are manufactured.

**Segment specialization.** A prior Dirac-3 campaign found in-segment wins for this family on non-fraud benchmarks (single seed, small margins). The preregistered test replicates them with seeds and intervals, then tests transfer to fraud segments against a matched random-segment control.

**Scale and sparsity, now measured rather than projected.** We ran the preregistered IEEE-CIS protocol: 590,540 transactions, 3.5% prevalence, rolling-origin evaluation by month, the reduced feature recipe, and the shuffled-label control, which collapses on every fold. Classical arms reach 0.5739 AUPRC at that prevalence [SIM]. We state what those arms are: hyperparameters carried from the tuned ULB settings as a hypothesis, not a per-dataset search, since the section 6 100-trial budget on IEEE-CIS has not been spent. The classical bar is therefore a floor and may rise. The CVQBoost arm, held to the six features the free-tier variable ceiling permits, reaches 0.0571 -- but a matched control settles what that means: the same gradient-boosted model given the SAME six features falls from 0.5424 to 0.0734, so every model is starved there, and the quantum arm attains 85% of that constrained ceiling. The preregistered feature ladder then asked whether lifting the restriction closes the gap, and the answer is no: across k in {5, 9, 13, 17} both arms improve and the classical arm improves faster, a slope of -0.006 AUPRC per feature [SIM] (Appendix A.5). We report that plainly because it removes the convenient explanation. On a wide engineered feature space a weighted vote over one- and two-feature weak learners does not represent the interactions a boosted tree exploits. What the ladder does not test is the formulation that would: the cardinality-constrained integer problem of section 6 and three-feature subsets, neither yet run. The phase representation HAS now been tested and does not move the delta (A.6). This is a boundary on one configuration, not a verdict.

**Training time against sample count, the vendor's actual claim.** CVQBoost's published claim is a runtime advantage at competitive accuracy on synthetic sets from one million to seventy million samples, so our accuracy null at 284,000 rows is consistent with that reporting rather than contrary to it: accuracy was never the claim. The sharper experiment we have not run is end-to-end training time against sample count including Hamiltonian construction, whose classical cost grows with the square of the pool size and decides whether the scaling claim survives.

**The formulation where the optimizer would have to work.** Our convexity result is the strongest argument for the next experiment: the continuous relaxation is classically solvable, so the interesting problem is the combinatorial one it relaxes. Choosing the best sparse subset of weak learners under a cardinality constraint is NP-hard in general, and it is the native problem class for Dirac-3's integer solver. NP-hardness bears on worst-case general instances; it does not establish that a quantum device is necessary, or advantageous, on the finite instances we would run, and we claim neither. At the sizes we would test first, a commercial mixed-integer solver may still certify a global optimum, so the preregistered controls for that experiment are a time-capped MIQP solve alongside greedy and annealing heuristics. A win against a certified-optimal classical solve is a result; a win against no control is not. That is the Phase 2 experiment we would run first, and the reason hardware access matters to it.

The configurations here were sized to a free-tier variable limit, not to the problem; Appendix B gives the bounds.
