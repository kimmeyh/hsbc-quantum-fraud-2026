## Quantum-native feature engineering (the representation argument)

**1. Mancilla, J. and Tagliani, T. "The Fourier Wall: Why Public Tabular Datasets Refuse Quantum Advantage, and a Certified Recipe for Where It Lives." arXiv:2607.15815.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15815 (PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.15815). Local: `docs/papers/fourier-wall-2607.15815.pdf`.
Full-paper summary: an angle-encoded QNN is a partial Fourier series, so quantum advantage requires five simultaneous conditions (C1 off-grid spectrum, ratio > 0.3; C2 joint interactions of order >= 3; C3 near-independent encoded features, since correlation dequantizes; C4 tree-breaking oscillation frequency; C5 a joint spectrum dense beyond enumeration, the condition most claims never test). SPECTRA is their two-tier certificate: tier 1 screens in minutes with no quantum simulation; tier 2 trains the quantum lane against a COMPLETE classical bar: logistic, trained-frequency GAM, pairwise GA2M, gradient-boosted trees, and the order-matched JOINT twin (supervised search over k-way cosine terms, a dozen parameters, seconds to fit). Key measured results: real operational targets are refused (trees at 0.999); a process-generated label on real energy phases certifies (quantum 0.994 vs complete-bar 0.699); phase features beat PCA representations of the same information by +0.217 (every PCA lane near chance); entanglement in the ENCODING is load-bearing (+0.136) while a variational-only entangler is null; an omitted JOINT twin would have converted a classical 0.968 into a fake quantum win at 0.758. Exact phase recipe (section 6.4): rank phase phi = 2*pi*(rank(v) - 1/2)/n - pi, log-transform magnitudes first, calendar fields mapped to their cycle, whiten the encoded block and re-rank to uniform, all fit on covariates only. Route-and-blend deployment: router gate must be independent of the specialist's features, and the deployed specialist must exactly match the certified configuration (an undertrained variant turned the blend negative). Honest scope: the certified win is on a semi-synthetic process-generated label; the certified benefit at simulable widths is resource separation (exact simulation cost grows 2^1.14n, crossover n* ~ 13-19 sites), not accuracy. SPECTRA the certificate is proprietary Falcondale technology.

**4. Zajac, S., Cybulski, J.L., Dziewit, B., Kulpa, T. "The Inverse Born Rule Equivalence." arXiv:2602.21350v2.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.21350 (PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.21350v2). Local: `docs/papers/inverse-born-rule-2602.21350v2.pdf`.
Full-paper summary: Equivalence Theorem: any REAL-valued amplitude encoding (probability loading included) composed with a data-independent unitary and measurement yields exactly the class of classical quadratic forms (the RKHS of an inner-product/Hellinger kernel), computable classically in O(N^2). Mechanism: real amplitudes force the Berry connection to zero, collapsing Fubini-Study geometry to Fisher-Rao. Three cheap diagnostics: phase complexity C (imaginary norm fraction; C = 0 iff all amplitudes real), Berry connection magnitude, and mode mutual information. Critical experimental detail: plain ANGLE ENCODING with Ry rotations and CNOTs also has C = 0 (real amplitudes), and so does Ry-based data re-uploading; they perform well via the trigonometric basis and depth, but carry no Type B quantum resources. Genuinely quantum encodings require complex phases: sandwich encoding (RY, e^{-i lambda ZZ}, RY) or Hamiltonian encoding, which show C > 0 and win XOR at fewer parameters. Two escape routes from the classical bound: complex phases, or data re-uploading (breaks the data-independent-ansatz premise).

## Hybrid architecture and data efficiency

**5. Rehman, S., Innan, N., Shafique, M. "QSTAR: Quantum Selective Transfer with Adaptive Routing." arXiv:2607.21411.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21411 (PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.21411). Code: https://github.com/sr7849-sudo/QSTAR. Local: `docs/papers/qstar-2607.21411.pdf`.
Full-paper summary: confidence-threshold routing: keep the classical prediction when max softmax confidence >= tau, route the rest to a fallback head. At tau = 0.70 (validation-selected), 39.6% of samples route; the searched quantum fallback beats a parameter-matched MLP fallback on routed samples by 6.82/4.31/3.03 points at tau = 0.70/0.80/0.90, and the full adaptive quantum system beats the adaptive classical baseline by 2.7 points (80.9% vs 78.2%), though the best fixed classical head (81.6%) still edges the adaptive system. Honest limitations stated: single seed, no confidence intervals, and a negative generalization check (Ants & Bees) where routing does not help. Their control design (parameter-matched fallback under the identical routing rule) is the right attribution pattern.

**6. Sakhnenko, A., Mendl, C.B., Lorenz, J.M. "Is data-efficient learning feasible with quantum models?" arXiv:2508.19437v2.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19437 (PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.19437). Local: `docs/papers/data-efficient-qml-2508.19437.pdf`.
Full-paper summary: a semi-artificial data-generation tool that relabels real tabular features to saturate target-alignment for a quantum kernel; on such datasets quantum kernel methods reach a given error with visibly fewer training samples than RBF kernels, the first controlled empirical demonstration of data-efficient quantum learning on classical features. The spectral-bias generalization metric (Canatar et al.) tracks empirical QKM performance closely, unlike earlier complexity/expressivity metrics. Analysis of the generated labeling functions suggests quantum kernels favor Lipschitz-smooth functions with specific curvature. Caveat for citation: labels are artificial by construction; this is evidence about which function classes favor quantum models, not about natural datasets.

## Design implications adopted from the FULL-paper reads (feeds PREREGISTRATION v1.1)

1. **QFE recipe is now exact:** use the Fourier Wall section 6.4 pipeline verbatim (rank phases, log magnitudes, calendar cycles, train-only whitening, low-cardinality phases kept OUT of any encoded block). Applies identically to all arms.
2. **The classical bar for H6 must include the order-matched twin.** If we publish a phase-feature quantum result without a JOINT-style twin (logistic over supervised-searched k-way cosine terms, seconds to fit), the very paper we cite documents how that inflates quantum wins (0.758 fake vs 0.968 real). Add trained-frequency GAM/GA2M/JOINT as cheap additional classical arms in any H6 cell.
3. **The Braket gate-based arm must be phase-active.** Plain Ry angle encoding has zero phase complexity and is provably a classical quadratic form (Inverse Born Rule); a reviewer who knows this paper would call an Ry-encoded arm "quantum" in name only. Use sandwich or entangling encoding, and REPORT the C / Berry-connection / mode-MI diagnostics of our encoding as evidence the quantum framing is credible (rubric criterion 2 asks exactly this).
4. **Route-and-blend constraints for the Hybrid Integration section:** the router must gate on features independent of the specialist's inputs, and the deployed specialist must match the evaluated configuration exactly. Cite QSTAR for confidence-threshold routing with a parameter-matched fallback control, and the Fourier Wall for the blend architecture; note QSTAR's single-seed caveat when citing its numbers.
5. **Framing discipline:** the Fourier Wall's certified win is semi-synthetic and its stated benefit is resource separation; Sakhnenko's data-efficiency is on relabeled targets. Neither supports "quantum beats classical on natural fraud data"; both support the conditions-based regime framing. Cite them for mechanism and method, never for headline performance.

Citation rule: references 1 to 6 are cited in the technical approach and references; SPECTRA datasets cited wherever B4 replication results appear.
